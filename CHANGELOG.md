# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented here. The format is based
on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and this project
uses [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- Three new levels between Jordbro and Södertörns villastad, in the
  order the commuter train runs: BANA 5, Handen in 1971 (the wooden
  station house of 1901, Övre Rudan and its bathing raft, Handens centrum and Nämndhuset,
  the new Haninge kommun's brick tower, Folkets hus on the esker
  Söderbymalm, Rudans gård and the water tower); BANA 6, Skogås in the
  early 1980s (the open-air centre and the fire station, a point
  block, Skogåshallen's pool, Lännafortet of 1914 and
  Beatebergsanstalten); and BANA 7, Trångsund on 1 May 1969 (the
  narrow sound in Drevviken, the tall block and Tacksägelsekyrkan, the
  motorway protests at Fållan, Bellmanslindarna and Ågestaverket, the
  nuclear plant in the rock, its cooling tower leaking). Each has two
  hidden rooms, six power-ups and a song of its own: a dance band's
  foxtrot, new wave, and a surf guitar's spy theme. Södertörns
  villastad is now BANA 8.
- Every level has its own music: an unhurried evening visa in Tungelsta,
  the garden town, with a harp picking under it; fife and drum in
  Krigslida; 1970s disco funk in Västerhaninge; a machine of a song in
  Jordbro, a sequencer running under a cold tune; and a summer hit with
  calypso bass and steel drums in Södertörns villastad. Förorten keeps
  the old suburbs' theme.
- The first of ten new characters, the subcultures, to try out on
  `?bana=figurer` (they belong to no level yet): the hippie, who wanders
  with his guitar and sits down to strum it, notes rising. Near him
  everything is mellow and Blenke walks slower. Put out of action, he
  gives away the sign the police's brochure looked for (LÅNGT HÅR) and
  drops a bag of herbal tea, logged as evidence.
- The headbanger: he bangs his head to his cassette player and, when
  Blenke comes near, stage-dives at him in a long arc, then gets up and
  bangs on. Sign: LYSSNAR PÅ HÅRDROCK; he drops a cassette.
- The punk: she pogos, bouncing high on the spot and now and then
  sideways. Her mohawk is sharp, so landing on her on the way up hurts;
  stomp her at the bottom of a bounce. Sign: SÄKERHETSNÅLAR; she drops a
  fanzine.
- The synth: he moves like a machine, a beat walking and a beat standing,
  and plays arpeggios that leave his keytar as square waves, stepping up
  and down as they fly. Sign: SVART SMINK; he drops a floppy disk.
- The breakdancer: he spins on his head on a piece of cardboard, his legs
  flying, and landing on him then hurts; between spins he freezes with
  his arms crossed (FRYS!, and a record scratch), and only then can he be
  stomped. Sign: KEPS; he drops a mixtape.
- The raver: she dances, hopping to the beat, and blows her whistle as
  she lobs glow sticks towards Blenke. They hurt nobody: they lie glowing
  a while and show what is hidden near them. Sign: VISSELPIPA; she drops
  a water bottle.
- The goth: he stands unseen in the shadows until Blenke comes close,
  then fades in with a sigh and drifts slowly towards him, and fades
  again once Blenke has gone. Unseen, he can neither hurt nor be hurt.
  Sign: KLÄR SIG I SVART; he drops a candle.
- The raggare: he cruises slowly back and forth in a big red 1950s
  American car, a beer in hand. Its roof carries Blenke like a moving
  platform (SKÅL, KONSTAPELN! and a honk), its side hurts, and he can't
  be beaten, by a cone either: alcohol is the legal drug. He doesn't
  count in the police report.
- The progg activist: she marches back and forth with a banner held high
  on a pole, and Blenke can ride on the banner. Under it she can't be
  stomped (landing on her hurts); only a thrown cone beats her, and her
  banner goes down with her. Sign: POLITISKT ENGAGEMANG; she drops a
  stencilled leaflet.
- The skater: he rolls fast and ollies over gaps, kerbs and Blenke.
  Stomped, he falls off and his board rolls on by itself for a while, and
  Blenke can ride it. Sign: VIDA BYXOR; he drops a can of spray paint.

- BANA 5: SÖDERTÖRNS VILLASTAD, set in the 1970s and
  80s where the villa town of 1901 stood. From the station that still
  bears its name, down under the tracks (or over them, which is
  forbidden), past the slab block that replaced the villas, through the
  yellow wooden villas left by Magelungen (a study circle on the
  drug-free society upstairs, something growing in the cellar), across
  Farsta strandbad on the jetty, pedal boats and the diving tower, to the
  tunnelbana's last stop, where the live track has to be jumped. Seven
  new signs to read, and the station's own name board.
- BANA 3: VÄSTERHANINGE: the station house coming down as the
  commuter trains arrive, the centre of 1969 cut open with a car park on
  its roof, Nynäsvägen dug up, a laundry in Håga that washes everything
  but money, Ribbyskolan's drug education, and the prosecutor waiting at
  the old courthouse, moved fifteen metres for the road.
- BANA 2: KRIGSLIDA, in 1980: the new halt in the fields, a hill of
  ancient graves with its runestone, the gravel pit, Lidakyrkan, new
  the autumn before where the mission house Elimsborg stood (its old
  cellar still under the floor), the fields of the referendum's three
  lines, Krigslida farm being searched, and the front line of the war on
  drugs. Every station
  now shows its own name on its board.
- BANA 4: JORDBRO, in the 1970s:
  the commuter trains' new platform and its walkway tunnel, the grave
  field the railway cuts through, a slab block of 1963 with a flat
  someone would rather keep hidden, a footbridge over the old
  Nynäsvägen, the low shops of Jordbro centrum round Hurtigs torg,
  Bröderna Hedlunds Rörverk with its leaking pipes and a pickling bath,
  and the gravel pits where Stockholm's airport nearly went. Seven signs,
  and three phone booths on the way.
- BANA 1: TUNGELSTA, the garden town: Boberg's
  yellow station house, the plots AB Hem på Landet sold to gardeners,
  Rosenborgs handelsträdgård's greenhouses of sweet peas and their
  boiler chimney, the electric society's transformer tower (whose
  cellar uses more power than a greenhouse should), the old fire station
  and the church's bell tower, and a hill at Hanveden's edge to see the
  moon from.

### Changed

- The park bench stands on proper cast-iron legs, its seat at knee
  height, and the bin shed has given up sorting: one big green
  container, overflowing with sacks, an old TV, a bicycle wheel, bottles
  and a mattress, as rubbish was thrown out in the seventies.
- Each level shows a backdrop that fits its place and its years. The
  Globe, built 1986–89, no longer stands on the horizon of the levels

  villastad), and Tungelsta and Krigslida, fields, greenhouses and
  forest, have no blocks of flats behind them: only red cottages and
  trees. Förorten and the title keep the suburb as it is now.

  levels numbered simply BANA 1 to 5: Tungelsta, Krigslida,
  Västerhaninge, Jordbro and Södertörns villastad. Förorten, the first

  by the asset factory. The HUD's icons for samples, possession and the
  power-up at work are the same pictures, and a power-up still glows
  round its own outline.
- The HUD's hearts and Blenke's head for the lives are new too, the head
  in his 1970s peaked cap like the rest of him, and so is the page's
  icon.

## [0.4.0] - 2026-11-08

### Added

- The game can be played with the fingers on a phone or tablet: a pad on
  the left walks left and right and crouches (pushed out towards its rim
  Blenke runs), and on the right a big button jumps and a smaller one
  helps, reads, lifts and throws; a button in the corner pauses. They
  show from the start on a touch screen, and on a computer once the
  screen is touched, and go away again at a key. Sound starts at the
  first touch.
- The menus and screens can be tapped, or clicked: a tapped choice is
  taken, the volumes are set by tapping their bars, and a tap goes on
  wherever the game waits for Enter. Played by touch the screens say
  "TRYCK PÅ SKÄRMEN" instead of naming keys, the controls screen tells
  the touch controls, the sound settings get a line to turn all sound
  off and on, and the prompt over a citizen or sign names no key.
- On a phone the game goes fullscreen and turns sideways at the first
  touch, where the browser allows it. Where it can't (an iPhone), a
  phone held upright is asked to turn ("VÄND TELEFONEN") and the play
  waits under the pause menu until it is. Where a tap turns it, an
  upright phone is told so ("TRYCK PÅ SKÄRMEN") until the first touch.
- A police station stands past the goal of world 1-1, with a filling
  station outside. Once the level is completed Blenke walks up to it by
  himself, plugs in his hose and pours every urine sample he carries into
  it: the PROV counter drains while the needle on the pump's gauge swings
  from 0 towards MAX (all the samples the level holds) and stops where
  his haul puts it, with a gurgle, a click and a ding (a little fanfare
  for a full tank).
- The police performance report pays a urine bonus on its own line under
  the time bonus: 20 points for every sample poured into the filling
  station. A full tank (the needle at MAX) doubles it once it has been
  counted in, with a fanfare.

### Changed

- A high score is signed with a three-letter tag, arcade style, in big
  letters: ↑ and ↓ turn a letter, ← and → move between the three, and
  Enter moves on and signs from the last; the letters can be typed too.
  By touch, tap the arrows over and under each letter and KLAR. The
  made-up officers on a new list sign with tags of their own, and the
  names on a list saved before are cut to their first three letters.
- A find is gone the moment it is picked up, instead of leaping up and
  blinking out; its sparkle and points still show.
- Blenke, the enemies, the citizens and everything else on a lawn now
  stand in the grass: its blades reach up in front of their feet. Pickups
  lie on top of the blades, so even the small ones show whole.
- The backdrop draws far faster on large screens: on a 4K canvas without
  a graphics card the game went from about 32 to 53 frames a second.
- The F2 panel shows how long the game spends stepping and drawing each
  frame.
- Power-ups stand out from the plain finds: they glow round their outline
  in shifting hi-vis yellow and blue light, and glitter in the same
  colours (the police coffee no longer looks like any cup of coffee).
- The parking lot in world 1-1 has one police car instead of three.
- Every enemy is redrawn by hand at the size it had: the stoner with his
  beanie and smouldering joint, the hooded dealer in shades and a gold
  chain, the party people in three loud colours punching the air, the
  bureaucrat holding out his form, the technician raising a test tube,
  the ideologue shouting into a megaphone and the prosecutor lifting his
  binder of charges.
- What the enemies throw is redrawn by hand at the size it had: the
  bureaucrat's form turns over as it flutters down, the technician's test
  tube and the prosecutor's binder tumble through the air, his document
  flaps along the floor and the ideologue's shout flashes. Everything
  thrown faces the way it flies, so a shout's tail points back at the
  one who shouted.
- Ranks are earned by points rather than by urine samples, so every stomp,
  find and bonus counts towards the next promotion. The made-up officers
  on a new high score list carry the ranks their points earn.

### Fixed

- While the level stands still after a lost life, at the goal or at the
  game over, a patch of slime, the canal or a pipe could keep bubbling,
  glinting or spraying every frame; now the surroundings rest too.
- Losing the last heart to one enemy no longer lets Blenke stomp another
  in the same moment, scoring points and a bounce as he goes down.
- A key pressed just as the window lost focus no longer stays held, with
  Blenke running on by himself when you come back.

### Development

- `src/touch.js` lays out the touch controls and turns fingers into touch
  keys for the input (`TOUCH_ACTIONS`); `src/art/touch.js` draws them.
  Taps on the screens are resolved by `tapAt` in `src/screens.js` and
  reach the game as the input's taps.
- The save is version 3, with a migration from version 2 cutting names to
  tags; Enter no longer means `submit`.
- The spec says phones play by touch and scores are signed with tags.
- Fullscreen and the sideways lock are asked for as the finger lifts, the
  only moment a browser counts a touch as the go-ahead (found on a Pixel 8).
- The mobile portion is done after the Android try; the iPhone check is
  left in the plan's Features.
- `src/fringe.js` says which lawn tiles grow a fringe, how many rows it
  takes and how far pickups are lifted above it; a test holds the delivered and the code-drawn lawn to leaving no
  gap under it.
- `src/backdrop.js` splits each backdrop layer into bands to fill or draw
  in columns; a test redraws every layer from its bands, pixel for pixel.
- `outline` in `src/pixels.js` rings a picture in a colour; tests hold the
  power-ups' glow to their outlines and their glitter to themselves.
- `src/station.js` runs the filling station and `src/art/station.js` draws it; tests step it through and hold every level of the game to one.
- The verify skill's Chromium profile folder is named after blenkehusis.
- The factory's game folder is `games/blenkehusis` now; the comments and docs point there.
- The asset factory's enemy sheets are delivered and drawn by their own origin (`ENEMY_SPRITES` in `src/sprites.js`); tests hold them to the code-drawn enemies' height.
- The asset factory's projectile sheets are delivered and drawn by their own origin (`PROJECTILE_SPRITES` in `src/sprites.js`); tests hold them to the code-drawn size, centred.
- TODO.md plans a filling station at the end of each level, with a urine bonus.
- TODO.md plans fewer police cars, a countryside level and lower flats for the suburb.
- TODO.md leaves the code-drawn fallback for delivered art out of step until after the code-review audit.
- TODO.md plans portion 10: playing on a phone, with touch controls.
- TODO.md plans the rest of the art requests to the asset factory, in five batches.
- World 1-1's balance is accepted as it is for now, after the owner played it.
- The full code-review audit's findings are in `docs/code-review-2026-11-08-findings.md`.
- The audit's BUG-3 (every printable key held back from the browser) is won't-fix, and the playbook says why.
- The audit's code-quality findings are tidied: an unused import, a doubled clock step and a redundant ternary.
- The code-review audit is done; the release is what is left of the polish portion.

## [0.3.0] - 2026-11-08

### Added

- Sound, chiptune style: Blenke's jumps, landings (harder the further he
  falls) and bumps of the head, and a sound for each kind of find:
  urine samples ping, evidence beeps, bonus items arpeggiate and minor
  possession gets a small fanfare. A combo of pickups climbs the scale.
  Sound starts with the first key press and stops while the tab is
  hidden.
- Sounds for the rest of the level: getting hurt, the paperwork shield
  taking a hit, a life lost (a sad tune, a slide whistle into a pit, a
  sinking wobble in the water, a buzzer when time is up), stomping and
  striking enemies, the prosecutor's gavel as he wakes and the thud of
  each hit, crates splitting, every power-up with its own sound (coffee
  rush, stamp, scanner, ka-ching, gurgle, rotor) and one as it runs out,
  promotions, helping a citizen (half-hearted), signs, cones, secret
  rooms, checkpoints and the barrier lifting. Enemies throwing, dropping
  and shouting are heard when they are on screen.
- The clock sounds an alarm at 30 seconds left and ticks through the
  last ten.
- Sounds on the screens: the menus' mark moving and a choice taken,
  pausing and going on, moving on from a report or the game over, each
  letter typed for the high scores (and a buzz for one the name won't
  take), and the time bonus ticking into the score with a ka-ching at
  the end.
- Music: the title screens play a jaunty chiptune march, the police band
  at a ribbon cutting, with a bridge stamping its notes in triplicate.
- World 1 has its own bouncy theme, and the prosecutor a courtroom drama
  far too grand for 0.02 grams while he fights. Reaching the goal plays a
  short fanfare, the reports come with lift music, the game over slides
  sadly down, and a score that makes the list gets a fanfare of its own.
  The music stops while a lost life passes and starts again with Blenke.
- Pausing turns the music down, and leaving the window silences the game
  until you come back.
- M turns all sound off and on, on any screen but the name entry; a
  struck-through loudspeaker in the corner shows it is off.
- LJUD on the title and pause menus sets the music's and the effects'
  volume, ten steps each, with ← and →. The controls screen lists M.
- The sound settings are saved with the high scores, once you have said
  yes to saving; you are never asked for them alone.
- Particles: dust from Blenke's feet as he jumps, lands hard and skids
  round at a run; stars and a puff of smoke from a stomped enemy,
  splinters from a crate, a sparkle in each find's colours, stars when
  he is hurt, shredded forms when
  the paperwork shield takes a hit, a ring of sparkles at a promotion and
  confetti at the goal. The prosecutor sheds paperwork with every hit
  and goes out in a storm of forms.
- Anything falling into the canal or the slime splashes, with a sound:
  Blenke, a cone, a dropped bag.
- The surroundings stir: slime bubbles, the canal glints, and a leaking
  pipe sprays where its stream lands and drips between leaks.
- Blows land with weight: the world stops for a split second when Blenke
  stomps or strikes an enemy or is hurt, longer for each hit on the
  prosecutor and longest when he is beaten. A jump pressed in that
  moment still counts.
- The view shakes when the prosecutor is hit and beaten, as the barrier
  lifts, when Blenke is hurt, as a crate splits and on the hardest
  landings. It stays still if your system is set to reduce motion.
- A find leaps up from where it was and blinks out instead of vanishing.
  The score and the count it adds to hop on the HUD, a heart lost
  shakes as it greys, a life lost hops the lives, and a phone booth
  jolts as it lights up.
- Screen transitions: a level opens with an iris on Blenke, and the iris
  closes on him after a lost life (opening again where he starts over),
  at the goal before the report and before the game over. The menus,
  reports and lists fade in from black in a pixel dither; pausing is
  still at once.

### Changed

- Blenke has a new look from the asset factory: a dark blue uniform with
  a white strap across it.
- The parked police cars are the asset factory's: a white saloon with
  POLIS along its side and the blue and yellow stripes.
- The Globe rises on the horizon behind the blocks of flats, drifting
  past slower than they do; the water tower among the flats is gone.
- The title menu has LJUD, before Topplista, and the pause menu has it
  between Fortsätt and Avsluta. The consent question says the sound
  settings are saved too.
- The checkpoints are phone booths, where Blenke calls in his status
  report: dark until he passes, then lit.

### Development

- A chip synthesizer that renders sounds as sample data, with tests on
  every sound's start, peak, end and level; cues map the game's events to
  them, and a test fails on an event that is neither cued nor listed
  silent. Songs are written as text and rendered into seamless loops,
  a few voices a frame.
- A listening page, like the asset factory's for sprites:
  `node tools/audition.js <dir>` writes every effect and song as WAV
  files at the game's levels, with a page showing each one's waveform,
  numbers and checks (now shared with the tests in `src/soundcheck.js`).
- The save is version 2, with the sound settings; a migration brings a
  version 1 save up with the default settings, and a test loads one.
- Particles are data in the world (`src/particles.js`), on their own
  seed; a test fails on a world event that neither bursts nor is listed
  without one. The world's events say where they happened; the
  surroundings stir tile by tile on the world's clock, only near the view.
- A test holds a finished pickup's leap ended however far behind the
  world the drawing is.
- `node tools/balance.js` prints a level's numbers for balancing it:
  finds and points, how far the samples go up the ranks, the clock and
  the time bonus, the boss, and the checkpoints against the dangers.
- The hit-stop and the shake are a table by event in `src/impact.js`,
  held to every world event like the particles and the cues; the tests
  share one scan of the events the modules send.
